> Astrocartography (ACG) takes the exact sky at his birth and asks, for every place on Earth, "which planet was rising, setting, culminating, or at the lowest point of the sky at that same instant?" Where a planet was angular, its themes get amplified in that location. Nothing in the birth chart changes; only which parts of it get turned up. This is a symbolic lens for reflection, not a substitute for the real drivers of a good move (work, visa, community, cost, climate, health, and the people you love). Use it to choose among places that already make practical sense, or to understand why a place felt the way it did.

## How to read the map

- **MC and IC lines are vertical** (meridians of longitude). The MC line is where a planet was at the top of the sky (career, visibility, public life). The IC line is the opposite meridian, where it was at the bottom (home, roots, private life, foundations).
- **ASC and DESC lines are curves.** The ASC line is where a planet was rising on the eastern horizon (it colors your identity, body, and how you meet the world). The DESC line is where it was setting (it colors partnership and the people you attract).
- **Orb (how close counts):** distances below are great-circle kilometers from the city to the line. Roughly, under ~150 km means "living on the line" (strong), 150 to 350 km is clearly in orb, and 350 to 700 km is a background influence.

## Caveats

- **Symbolic tool.** ACG describes tendencies and atmospheres, not guarantees. A place still has to work in real life.
- **Birth-time sensitivity.** The lines depend on the exact minute of birth. His 12:01 AM time is precise; if it turns out to be rounded, the meridian lines shift about 1° (roughly 100 km) for every 4 minutes, and the curved lines shift similarly. The broad regions hold, but a pinpoint "on the line" claim for a specific city could move by a city or two. Worth confirming the certificate time if he wants to act on a close call.
- **Distances** are great-circle kilometers from city to nearest line, computed from the Swiss Ephemeris positions. Only the ten planets and the North Node are mapped (the standard ACG set).
