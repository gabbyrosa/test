How this document is set

Three typographic registers run throughout, and they mark three kinds of information, not three astrological layers. Jyotisha does not supply a three-part hierarchy and none is implied here.

Monospace is computation: degrees, padas, virupas, dates, anything checkable against an ephemeris. Serif is the traditional interpretive voice, what the classical sources hold. Sans is explanation in my own words, including every statement about what a placement might look like in a life.

One caveat that has to come first

Bhava chalit divergence

The Lagna falls at 22°16′, very late in Virgo. Under whole-sign bhavas a graha’s rashi is its house. Under Sripati cusps, which many Jyotishis use to decide where a graha actually sits, the cusps land near 22° of each sign, so anything in the early degrees of a rashi drops back a house.

Six of nine grahas shift. Moon 6→5. Mars 9→8. Mercury 9→8. Jupiter 4→3. Saturn 7→6. Rahu 1→12. Ketu 7→6. Only the Sun and Venus hold.

This reading uses whole sign throughout, which is the Parashari standard and the convention nearly all classical yoga rules assume. But a late Lagna makes that choice load-bearing rather than incidental, and where a conclusion would reverse under the other convention, this document says so instead of presenting one reading as settled.

02

Lagna and Lagna lord

Technical

Lagna 22°16′00″ Virgo, in Hasta nakshatra, pada 4, whose lord is the Moon. Hasta pada 4 falls in the Pisces navamsa. Lagna lord is Mercury, placed in Taurus in the 9th bhava, in a friend’s sign, direct in motion, 23°13′ from the Sun and therefore not combust. Mercury sits in Krittika pada 2, whose lord is the Sun. Mercury’s dispositor is Venus, in its own sign in the same bhava. Mercury receives one aspect, Saturn’s 3rd-house special drishti. It casts on the 3rd.

Technical fact

Lagna lord Mercury is in the 9th, conjunct Venus (lord of the 9th and 2nd), the Sun (12th lord) and Mars (3rd and 8th lord). The Lagna lord is therefore not in the Lagna, not in a kendra, and is instead concentrated in a trikona alongside four other bodies, with its own dispositor Venus in own sign beside it.

Classical delineation

A Lagna lord in the 9th is called well placed in classical Parashari terms: the 9th is the strongest trikona, the house of dharma, fortune, teachers, father and long journeys. The self is described as finding its footing through the 9th’s matters rather than through the 1st’s. That the Lagna lord also owns the 10th ties livelihood into the same package. Its placement with the 9th lord in the 9th lord’s own sign is what makes the raja yoga in section 9 possible.

Possible lived expressions

Plural and probabilistic, and the tradition does not pick between them:

  • A sense of self built more around belief, orientation or field of interest than around body or personality
  • Formative influence from teachers, mentors, older figures or a tradition, rather than self-invention
  • Identity that travels well, and may be clarified by distance from where one started
  • Work and worldview tending to fuse, so that a job feels like a stance rather than a task

Which of these dominates is a dasha question, not a natal one. See section 13.

The Lagna’s nakshatra chain

Hasta’s lord is the Moon. The Moon is in the 6th, in Shatabhisha, whose lord is Rahu. Rahu is in Hasta, in the 1st. This closes: Lagna → Moon → Rahu → back to Hasta. The nakshatra governing the rising degree is ruled by a graha whose own nakshatra is ruled by the graha sitting on that rising degree. Section 5 takes this apart properly, because it is the single most unusual structure in the chart.

The chain, traced

Moon → Aquarius → Saturn. The Moon’s sign lord is Saturn, which sits in the 7th bhava in Pisces. Saturn there has 56.6 of a possible 60 virupas of dig bala, close to the maximum any graha can hold, because the 7th is Saturn’s direction of strength. Saturn is also in its own nakshatra, Uttara Bhadrapada, and rises to exaltation in the navamsa. So the Moon’s dispositor is one of the best-supported bodies in the chart, and it is supported by measures that have nothing to do with rashi dignity.

Moon → Shatabhisha → Rahu. The nakshatra lord is Rahu, which sits two degrees from the Lagna in Hasta — and Hasta’s lord is the Moon. This is a nakshatra parivartana, a mutual exchange at the nakshatra level: the Moon occupies Rahu’s asterism while Rahu occupies the Moon’s. There is no rashi-level exchange anywhere in this chart. There are two at nakshatra level, and this is one of them.

Pada and navamsa. Shatabhisha pada 3 places the Moon in the Aquarius navamsa, the same sign it occupies in the rashi chart. The Moon is vargottama. In classical terms this is a real strength: what the Moon is in the birth chart, it remains in the ninth harmonic, without dilution.

Where the layers reinforce, and where they contradict

Reinforcing

Vargottama status, a dispositor with near-maximal directional strength, a nakshatra lord sitting on the Lagna, and a Sunapha yoga (Saturn in the 2nd from the Moon, which also rules out Kemadruma, the isolated-Moon affliction). Four independent supports.

Contradicting

The 6th bhava is a dusthana. The Moon receives no graha drishti at all — nothing aspects it. It has no conjunction. It is waning, at 57.4% lit, which places it barely inside the window classical texts call a strong Moon and on the declining side of it. And its nakshatra lord is Rahu, a graha with no sign dignity of its own.

Technical fact

A vargottama Moon, unaspected and unconjunct, in a dusthana by whole sign but in the 5th under Sripati cusps, in mutual nakshatra exchange with Rahu, disposed by a Saturn that is directionally the strongest body in the chart.

Classical delineation

Parashara treats an unaspected Moon as self-referring: the mind takes its cues from its own dispositors rather than from other grahas. Vargottama sustains that consistency across the harmonic. A 6th-house Moon is classically associated with service, health, contention and daily labour — the 6th being shatru bhava — while a 5th-house Moon reads entirely differently, toward intelligence and creative issue. That the two conventions disagree here is a genuine ambiguity in this chart, not a gap in the reading.

Possible lived expressions
  • Emotional processing that happens internally and arrives already resolved, since nothing in the chart aspects the Moon to modify it in transit through the psyche
  • A steady rather than volatile temperament, from vargottama, with the steadiness coming from Saturn’s structure rather than from ease
  • A pull toward being useful, toward work with a service or problem-solving shape, if the 6th reading holds
  • Or a pull toward creative and intellectual output, if the Sripati 5th holds
  • Some dependence on Rahu-flavoured novelty, unconventional interests or outsider positions for emotional interest, given the Moon–Rahu exchange

The 6th-versus-5th question cannot be settled from the chart alone. It is the kind of thing that dasha evidence discriminates, and the Moon’s own mahadasha does not arrive until 2087.

Graha by graha

Sun

Technical. 23°29′59″ Taurus, 9th bhava, Mrigashira pada 1, nakshatra lord Mars. Taurus is Venus’s sign and Venus is the Sun’s natural enemy, so the Sun sits in an enemy’s rashi. Direct. Navamsa Leo — own sign, a clear gain. Dig bala 49.5/60. Baladi avastha Kumara. Functional lord of the 12th. Conjunct Mars, Mercury and Venus; aspected only by Saturn’s 3rd-house drishti; casts on the 3rd.

Classical. The Sun as 12th lord for Virgo Lagna is a functional malefic by lordship, but placed in the 9th — the house of father, dharma and fortune — in the company of the chart’s two most dignified benefics. A 12th lord in a trikona is classically read as expenditure and dissolution being channelled into the 9th’s matters, which the tradition treats more kindly than a 12th lord elsewhere. Its own-sign navamsa means the Sun’s weakness is a rashi-level appearance that does not persist into D9.

Possible expressions. Ambivalence toward authority figures without hostility to them; a sense of self that does not announce itself; the possibility that time, money or energy goes abroad, into study, or into things that do not return directly; a father or father-figure connected to belief, distance or education. All of these are contingent; none is required.

Mercury

Technical. 0°16′53″ Taurus — the very first degree of the sign — 9th bhava, Krittika pada 2, nakshatra lord Sun. Friend’s sign. Direct, 23°13′ from the Sun, not combust. Navamsa Capricorn. Dig bala 12.7/60, the second lowest in the chart. Baladi avastha Mrita. Lord of the 1st and the 10th. Conjunct Sun, Mars, Venus; aspected by Saturn.

Classical. Lagna lord and 10th lord in one body, placed in the 9th with the 9th lord: this is the raja yoga engine of the chart, treated in section 9. But two counterweights are on the record. Mercury sits at 0°16′, a sandhi degree at the junction of signs, which classical texts read as a graha not fully established in its rashi. And its baladi avastha is Mrita, the weakest of the five. Neither cancels a yoga; both temper the ease with which it operates.

Possible expressions. Intelligence directed at meaning rather than mechanics; a working life shaped around what one believes about a field; skill in language, analysis or systems used in service of a larger orientation; a slow start in establishing professional footing relative to the underlying capacity. The gap between capacity and establishment is a dasha matter.

Venus

Technical fact

28°01′11″ Taurus, 9th bhava, Mrigashira pada 2, nakshatra lord Mars. OWN SIGN. But: retrograde; combust at 4°32′ from the Sun against a retrograde orb of 8°; dig bala 9.0/60, the lowest of any graha here; baladi avastha Bala (infant); and DEBILITATED in the navamsa, falling to Virgo. Lord of the 9th and the 2nd.

Classical delineation

This is the most important nuance in the chart and it cuts against the obvious reading. Venus holds the single best rashi dignity available — its own sign, as lord of the best trikona, in that trikona. By rashi alone it is the chart’s strongest body. By every other classical measure it is among the weakest: asta (combust), retrograde, minimal dig bala, infant avastha, and neecha in D9. Parashari method does not let rashi dignity overrule the rest; it requires all of it to be weighed. A graha strong in rashi and weak in varga is classically read as promising more than it delivers without effort, or as delivering in bursts tied to period rather than continuously.

Possible lived expressions
  • Real aesthetic capacity and real taste, which nonetheless may not feel reliably available or self-evident from the inside
  • Value, beauty and relationship mattering enormously as themes while being complicated in operation
  • Fortune arriving through periods rather than as a standing condition — and note that the current antardasha is Venus’s, running to December 2028
  • A gap between how much beauty matters to her and how much she experiences herself as having it
  • Retrogression classically turns a graha’s work inward or backward: revisiting, reworking, returning to things

None of this says the capacity is absent. It says the chart makes its availability conditional, and section 13 is where the conditions live.

Mars

Technical. 2°44′51″ Taurus, 9th bhava, Krittika pada 2, nakshatra lord Sun. Neutral sign. Direct. Not combust (20°45′). Navamsa Capricorn — exalted, a substantial gain. Dig bala 42.6/60. Baladi avastha Mrita. Functional lord of the 3rd and the 8th, both difficult houses for this Lagna, making Mars the clearest functional malefic here. Casts special drishti on the 4th (onto Jupiter) and the 12th, plus the 7th aspect on the 3rd.

Classical. A functional malefic sitting in the best trikona is a mixed signature: the 8th lord in the 9th is a classical difficulty (the 12th from the 9th, dissolving fortune) while placement in a trikona among benefics moderates it. Mars in Mrita avastha at rashi level but exalted in navamsa is the mirror image of Venus: weak in the visible chart, strong underneath.

Possible expressions. Drive that attaches to belief or to a field rather than to competition; a capacity for sustained effort that is more available in practice than it appears on paper; friction around inheritance, shared resources, or the fortunes of the father; energy that shows up under pressure rather than on demand.

Jupiter

Technical. 22°05′33″ Sagittarius, 4th bhava, Purva Ashadha pada 3, nakshatra lord Venus. OWN SIGN. Retrograde. Navamsa Libra — own-sign status is not retained. Dig bala 30.1/60. Baladi avastha Vriddha (old). Functional lord of the 4th and the 7th, both kendras. Receives Mars’s 8th-house drishti and Saturn’s 10th-house drishti. Casts on the 8th, 10th and 12th.

Kendradhipati dosha

For Virgo Lagna, Jupiter owns the 4th and the 7th and nothing else. A natural benefic owning only kendras acquires kendradhipati dosha in Parashari doctrine and functions closer to neutral or malefic for that Lagna. The 7th is additionally a maraka house. So the chart’s single Mahapurusha yoga is formed by a graha that is, for this specific Lagna, functionally compromised. Own-sign placement mitigates the dosha; it does not remove it.

Possible expressions. A strong attachment to home, land or domestic ground as a source of meaning; wisdom that arrives through the domestic and the private rather than the public; the possibility that expansion in one area of life reliably costs something in another, given the same graha rules both home and partnership. Jupiter’s mahadasha ran 2002 to 2018 — childhood through age twenty-two — so much of its work is already historical rather than pending.

Saturn

Technical fact

12°12′27″ Pisces, 7th bhava, Uttara Bhadrapada pada 3 — its own nakshatra. Neutral sign, no rashi dignity. Direct. Dig bala 56.6/60, the highest in the chart. Navamsa Libra — exalted. Vargottama in D10. Lord of the 5th (trikona) and the 6th (dusthana). Conjunct Ketu. Aspects the 1st, the 4th and the 9th — that is, the Lagna, Jupiter, and all four grahas in the ninth. Classical delineation

Saturn is the quiet structural centre of this chart, and nothing about its rashi says so. It holds no sign dignity at all, yet it has near-maximal directional strength, sits in its own nakshatra, exalts in the navamsa, is vargottama in the career varga, aspects more of the chart than any other graha, and rules a trikona. As 5th lord it carries the chart’s intelligence and creative signification; as 6th lord it carries service, health and difficulty. Saturn in the 7th is classically read as delay and weight in partnership, but also as durability once established, and dig bala there is a genuine strength rather than a consolation.

Possible lived expressions
  • Partnership arriving with structure, seriousness or an age or responsibility differential, and lasting
  • Creative and intellectual output that is disciplined rather than spontaneous, and improves with time
  • A long working relationship with limits, health, or service as recurring themes
  • Authority accumulating slowly and then holding

Because Saturn’s mahadasha runs to December 2037, these are live conditions now rather than background ones. Section 13 breaks the sub-periods down.

Classical delineation

Nakshatra lordship is the layer Vimshottari runs on, so these chains describe how periods will actually deliver. A chart whose nakshatra network converges on a Sun–Mars pair is described as operating through initiative, heat, visibility and assertion — the two most kshatriya grahas — regardless of how the rashi chart looks. That the emotional apparatus (Moon) sits outside that convergence, in a sealed pair with Rahu, is a genuine structural separation and not a minor detail.

Possible lived expressions
  • A public or working self that runs hotter and more directly than the emotional self does
  • An inner life that answers to different logic than the outer one, and does not translate easily into it
  • Interests, tastes or emotional attachments that feel unaccountable to the rest of the personality
  • Saturn’s self-reference suggesting a domain — discipline, structure, partnership, service — that answers to nothing but itself

Classical delineation

For Virgo Lagna the practical consequence is a reversal: Saturn is to be treated as broadly supportive and Jupiter as broadly complicating, in direct contradiction to their natural reputations. This is standard doctrine for this Lagna, not an idiosyncratic reading.

Possible lived expressions

  • Expansion, optimism and abundance carrying costs that are not obvious in advance
  • The house of home and the house of partnership sharing one ruler, so that the two areas move together whether or not that is convenient
  • Periods of Saturn reading better in life than they read on paper — and Saturn’s mahadasha is running now

Yogas actually present

Each rule is stated first, then tested. Combinations that do not form are listed too, because a yoga’s absence is information and its false presence is the commonest failure in software readings.

Hamsa Yoga — forms

Rule. One of the five Pancha Mahapurusha yogas: Jupiter in its own sign or exaltation, occupying a kendra from the Lagna.

Test. Jupiter in Sagittarius, its own sign, in the 4th bhava, a kendra. Forms.

Modification. Three qualifications, all on the record. Jupiter is retrograde. Jupiter carries kendradhipati dosha for Virgo Lagna. Jupiter receives the special drishti of both Mars and Saturn. And Jupiter does not retain own-sign status in the navamsa, falling to Libra. The yoga forms and is real; it is not unqualified.

Dharma-Karmadhipati Yoga — forms

Rule. Association of the 9th lord (dharma) with the 10th lord (karma). Classically among the strongest raja yogas available.

Test. 9th lord Venus and 10th lord Mercury are conjunct in Taurus in the 9th bhava. Venus is in its own sign there. Forms, by conjunction, in the 9th lord’s own house.

Modification. Venus is combust and debilitated in D9; Mercury sits at 0°16′ in sandhi and in Mrita avastha. The yoga is structurally excellent and its participants are individually compromised. Classical practice reads this as a yoga that delivers in its periods rather than continuously.

Lakshmi Yoga — forms, with one condition partially met

Rule. The 9th lord in its own sign or exaltation, in a kendra or trikona, with a strong Lagna lord.

Test. 9th lord Venus in own sign Taurus, in the 9th, a trikona — first condition fully met. Lagna lord Mercury in a friend’s sign in a trikona alongside it — well placed but not in own sign or exaltation. The primary condition forms; the supporting condition is met in placement but not in dignity.

Sunapha Yoga — forms

Rule. A graha other than the Sun in the 2nd from the Moon.

Test. Saturn in Pisces, the 2nd from the Moon in Aquarius. Forms. No graha occupies the 12th from the Moon, so Anapha does not form and Durudhara does not form.

Kemadruma — does not form

Rule. The affliction of the isolated Moon: no graha in the 2nd or 12th from it. Test. Saturn occupies the 2nd. Cancelled. This matters given how isolated the Moon otherwise is.

Strength, and its limits

What is not computed here, and why

Full Shadbala requires Sthana, Dig, Kala, Cheshta, Naisargika and Drik bala. Several of those — Cheshta bala in particular — have competing formulations across authorities, and the aggregate thresholds by which a graha is called strong or weak differ between schools. Producing a single number would mean picking one formulation and presenting it as settled. Shadbala is therefore not given.

Ishta and Kashta phala derive from Shadbala components and are likewise omitted rather than approximated.

Two measures with single unambiguous definitions are computed exactly: Dig bala, and Baladi avastha.

Technical fact

The two grahas with the best rashi dignity (Venus, own sign; Jupiter, own sign) rank last and fifth on directional strength. The graha with no rashi dignity at all (Saturn) ranks first, at 56.6 of 60. Mercury, the Lagna lord, ranks sixth.

Classical delineation

Parashari method treats these as independent axes that must both be weighed; neither overrules the other. A graha strong in sthana and weak in dig is classically read as having capacity without natural placement for exercising it — it holds the quality but not the position. The reverse, Saturn’s case, is a graha positioned to act without inherent quality to draw on: effective by location rather than by nature.

Possible lived expressions
  • Real talent in Venus’s domains that does not automatically convert into position, recognition or felt possession
  • Effectiveness in Saturn’s domains — structure, endurance, partnership, service — that exceeds any felt enthusiasm for them
  • A recurring gap between what is possessed and what is deployed

The question this section exists to answer

Does the apparent strength of Venus and Jupiter in the rashi chart persist in the navamsa? No, in both cases, and to different degrees.

Venus falls from own sign to debilitation. This is the largest single reversal in the chart. The body carrying the best rashi dignity, ruling the best trikona, and forming two of the three yogas, arrives in the navamsa neecha. Combined with combustion and the lowest dig bala in the chart, that is three measures pointing the same way, with infant avastha a minor fourth. But “weak everywhere except sign” would overstate it in two directions. Retrogression does not belong on that list at all: Shadbala assigns retrograde grahas high cheshta bala, so classically it counts as strength, and the modern reading of retrogression as weakening is not used here. And Venus recovers substantially in the topic-specific vargas — own sign in the 11th of D10, own sign and vargottama in D7. The accurate statement is that Venus is exceptionally strong in D1 sign dignity, materially weakened by several other natal strength indicators and by D9, and strong again in D10 and D7. Its expression is context-dependent rather than simply weak or strong, which is a more interesting result than either extreme.

Jupiter loses own-sign status, arriving in Libra as a neutral. The Hamsa yoga’s participant is not sustained in the second body. Less severe than Venus, but in the same direction.

Saturn exalts. The graha with no rashi dignity, maximal dig bala, its own nakshatra, and vargottama status in D10 also exalts in the navamsa. Across the non-rashi measures computed in this document, Saturn is the most consistently supported body in the chart.

Mars exalts in Capricorn, and the Sun gains its own sign. Both bodies that look mediocre in the rashi chart are substantially better underneath. This pairs with the nakshatra finding: the Sun–Mars loop that governs seven of nine placements consists of two grahas that are stronger in D9 than D1.

Rahu occupies the D9 Lagna in Cancer, as it occupies the D1 Lagna in Virgo. The node is on the ascending degree of both charts.

7th lord in D9

The D9 Lagna is Cancer, so its 7th is Capricorn, ruled by Saturn — which sits exalted in Libra in the 4th of D9. Capricorn itself holds Mars (exalted), Mercury and Ketu. The navamsa 7th is heavily occupied and its lord is exalted. Read alongside D1, where the 7th holds Saturn with maximal dig bala plus Ketu and is ruled by an own-sign Jupiter, both charts describe a 7th house that is structurally weighty rather than light.

Technical fact

Rahu sits 2°03′ from the ascending degree, in the same nakshatra and the same pada as the Lagna. Its nakshatra lord is the Moon; the Moon’s nakshatra lord is Rahu. Rahu also occupies the Lagna of the navamsa and of the D4. Ketu occupies the 7th with Saturn, disposed by an own-sign Jupiter.

Classical delineation

Parashara treats the nodes as chhaya grahas, shadow bodies with no substance of their own, which act through the graha that disposes them and through whatever they occupy. Rahu on the Lagna is classically described as amplifying and distorting the presentation of the self — magnifying the outward signal while loosening its correspondence to what is inside. Its dispositor here is Mercury, the Lagna lord, well placed in the 9th, which is a favourable channel. Ketu in the 7th with Saturn is read as detachment or non-attachment in the partnership house, with Saturn adding weight and duration to the same house.

I am not going to reach for past-life language. It exists in the later tradition rather than in the Parashari core this reading is built on, and using it would mean importing a framework I have not declared.

Synthesis by life area

Only now, and with each area separated into what the chart strongly supports, what is genuinely mixed, and what depends on period.

Identity and temperament

Strongly supported: a self constituted through orientation, belief and field rather than through self-assertion — Lagna lord in the 9th with the 9th lord, both major yogas seated there. A presentation that carries further than it feels — Rahu within 2° of the Lagna, in its pada, and on the Lagna of two other vargas.

Mixed: Mercury is the Lagna lord and is simultaneously the chart’s strongest functional benefic and one of its weakest bodies by dig bala and avastha. Capacity and position do not match.

Timing-dependent: Mercury’s own mahadasha does not begin until December 2037.

Emotional processing

Strongly supported: self-containment. The Moon receives no drishti from any graha, has no conjunction, and is vargottama. Whatever it does, it does without modification from the rest of the chart.

Mixed: the 6th-versus-5th bhava question is unresolved and unresolvable from the chart alone. Sunapha and the absence of Kemadruma are genuine supports; the dusthana placement and the waning phase are genuine pressures.

Structural, and worth stating plainly: the Moon sits outside the Sun–Mars nakshatra convergence that governs seven of nine placements, and reports to Jupiter through Saturn rather than to Venus. The emotional apparatus runs on different machinery than the rest of the chart. This appears independently at nakshatra level and at dispositor level.

Aesthetics and attraction

Strongly supported as a theme: Venus rules the 9th and the 2nd, sits in its own sign, and forms two of the three yogas. Beauty and value are structurally central to this chart.

Mixed, and this is the chart’s sharpest tension: Venus is combust, retrograde, lowest in dig bala, infant in avastha, and debilitated in navamsa. Five independent measures point one way and one points the other. The classical reading is capacity without easy access — not absence.

Timing-dependent: Venus’s antardasha is running now, to December 2028. On classical logic this is when its rashi dignity has its opening. Venus’s own mahadasha is not until 2061.
