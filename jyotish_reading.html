One caveat, and a correction to an earlier version of it

A convention error, now fixed

An earlier version of this document reported that six of nine grahas change bhava between whole sign and Sripati, and treated that as the reading’s largest vulnerability. That figure was wrong, and it was wrong because of a mistake in my own code.

I computed the cross-check by asking which cusp-to-cusp interval each graha fell into. That is the Western convention, in which a cusp begins a house. Jyotish does not use it. In bhava chalit the cusp is the bhava madhya, the midpoint, and the bhava runs from the sandhi halfway back to the previous madhya to the sandhi halfway on to the next. Treating a midpoint as a boundary displaces every graha by roughly half a bhava, which manufactured the divergence I then reported as a finding.

Bhava chalit, computed correctly

Under Sripati proper — Porphyry trisection of the quadrants for the madhyas, sandhis at the midpoints — two of nine grahas shift, not six. Mars 9→8 and Mercury 9→8. Everything else holds: Moon stays in the 6th, Jupiter in the 4th, Saturn and Ketu in the 7th, Rahu in the 1st, Sun and Venus in the 9th. Taking Placidus cusps as madhyas instead of Porphyry gives the same two.

The correction runs the other way from the caveat it replaces. Most of this chart’s structure is convention-independent, including the Hamsa yoga, Rahu on the Lagna, and the loaded 7th. The one thing genuinely at stake is how much of the ninth-house concentration survives, and section 16 tests every headline claim against it explicitly.

Lagna and Lagna lord

Technical

Lagna 22°16′00″ Virgo, in Hasta nakshatra, pada 4, whose lord is the Moon. Hasta pada 4 falls in the Pisces navamsa. Lagna lord is Mercury, placed in Taurus in the 9th bhava, in a friend’s sign, direct in motion, 23°13′ from the Sun and therefore not combust. Mercury sits in Krittika pada 2, whose lord is the Sun. Mercury’s dispositor is Venus, in its own sign in the same bhava. Mercury receives one aspect, Saturn’s 3rd-house special drishti. It casts on the 3rd.

Technical fact

Lagna lord Mercury is in the 9th, conjunct Venus (lord of the 9th and 2nd), the Sun (12th lord) and Mars (3rd and 8th lord). The Lagna lord is therefore not in the Lagna, not in a kendra, and is instead concentrated in a trikona alongside four other bodies, with its own dispositor Venus in own sign beside it.

Classical delineation

A Lagna lord in the 9th is called well placed in classical Parashari terms: the 9th is the strongest trikona, the house of dharma, fortune, teachers, father and long journeys. The self is described as finding its footing through the 9th’s matters rather than through the 1st’s. That the Lagna lord also owns the 10th ties livelihood into the same package. Its placement with the 9th lord in the 9th lord’s own sign is what makes the raja yoga in section 9 possible.

Possible lived expressions

Plural and probabilistic, and the tradition does not pick between them:

  • A sense of self built more around belief, orientation or field of interest than around body or personality
  • Formative influence from teachers, mentors, older figures or a tradition, rather than self-invention
  • Identity that travels well, and may be clarified by distance from where one started
  • Work and worldview tending to fuse, so that a job feels like a stance rather than a task

Which of these dominates is a dasha question, not a natal one. See section 13.

The Lagna’s nakshatra chain

Hasta’s lord is the Moon. The Moon is in the 6th, in Shatabhisha, whose lord is Rahu. Rahu is in Hasta, in the 1st. This closes: Lagna → Moon → Rahu → back to Hasta. The nakshatra governing the rising degree is ruled by a graha whose own nakshatra is ruled by the graha sitting on that rising degree. Section 5 takes this apart properly, because it is the single most unusual structure in the chart.

The chain, traced

Moon → Aquarius → Saturn. The Moon’s sign lord is Saturn, which sits in the 7th bhava in Pisces. Saturn there has 56.6 of a possible 60 virupas of dig bala, close to the maximum any graha can hold, because the 7th is Saturn’s direction of strength. Saturn is also in its own nakshatra, Uttara Bhadrapada, and rises to exaltation in the navamsa. So the Moon’s dispositor is one of the best-supported bodies in the chart, and it is supported by measures that have nothing to do with rashi dignity.

Moon → Shatabhisha → Rahu. The nakshatra lord is Rahu, which sits two degrees from the Lagna in Hasta — and Hasta’s lord is the Moon. This is a nakshatra parivartana, a mutual exchange at the nakshatra level: the Moon occupies Rahu’s asterism while Rahu occupies the Moon’s. There is no rashi-level exchange anywhere in this chart. There are two at nakshatra level, and this is one of them.

Pada and navamsa. Shatabhisha pada 3 places the Moon in the Aquarius navamsa, the same sign it occupies in the rashi chart. The Moon is vargottama. In classical terms this is a real strength: what the Moon is in the birth chart, it remains in the ninth harmonic, without dilution.

Where the layers reinforce, and where they contradict

Reinforcing

Vargottama status, a dispositor with near-maximal directional strength, a nakshatra lord sitting on the Lagna, and a Sunapha yoga (Saturn in the 2nd from the Moon, which also rules out Kemadruma, the isolated-Moon affliction). Four independent supports.

Contradicting

The 6th bhava is a dusthana. The Moon receives no graha drishti at all — nothing aspects it. It has no conjunction. It is waning, at 57.4% lit, which places it barely inside the window classical texts call a strong Moon and on the declining side of it. And its nakshatra lord is Rahu, a graha with no sign dignity of its own.

Technical fact

A vargottama Moon, unaspected and unconjunct, in a dusthana under both bhava conventions, in mutual nakshatra exchange with Rahu, disposed by a Saturn that is directionally the strongest body in the chart.

Classical delineation

An unaspected Moon has no delineation I can source. An earlier version of this document opened this paragraph with “Parashara treats an unaspected Moon as self-referring.” That attribution was invented. Section 17 then compounded the error by describing the phrasing as belonging to “an earlier version” while it was still present here. Both are corrected now, and the sequence is recorded rather than quietly repaired.

Provenance: inferred, mine. Within the declared framework the defensible statement is that no graha reaches the Moon by drishti, so its modification comes from its dispositors rather than from other bodies. Vargottama sustains whatever it is across the harmonic. A 6th-house Moon is associated in standard practice with service, health, contention and daily labour, the 6th being shatru bhava. An earlier version of this document treated the house as ambiguous between the 6th and the 5th; that was an artefact of the bhava error corrected in section 1, and the Moon holds in the 6th under both conventions.

Possible lived expressions
  • Emotional processing that is comparatively little modified by the rest of the chart, since under the declared drishti system no graha aspects the Moon. What that feels like from the inside is not something the configuration establishes
  • A steady rather than volatile temperament, from vargottama, with the steadiness coming from Saturn’s structure rather than from ease
  • A pull toward being useful, toward work with a service or problem-solving shape
  • Some dependence on Rahu-flavoured novelty, unconventional interests or outsider positions for emotional interest, given the Moon–Rahu exchange

None of these is established by the configuration; they are extrapolations from it. The Moon’s own mahadasha does not arrive until 2087, so no period evidence bears on them within a normal lifetime.

Sun

Technical. 23°29′59″ Taurus, 9th bhava, Mrigashira pada 1, nakshatra lord Mars. Taurus is Venus’s sign and Venus is the Sun’s natural enemy, so the Sun sits in an enemy’s rashi. Direct. Classical. The text declines to class the Sun for Virgo, saying its “role will depend on his association” (ch. 34 vv. 31–32). An earlier version of this reading called it a functional malefic on 12th lordship; that was inference, not doctrine. Its associations here are Mars, Mercury and Venus, and it is placed in the 9th — the house of father, dharma and fortune — in the company of the chart’s two most dignified benefics. A 12th lord in a trikona is classically read as expenditure and dissolution being channelled into the 9th’s matters, which the tradition treats more kindly than a 12th lord elsewhere. Its own-sign navamsa means the Sun’s weakness is a rashi-level appearance that does not persist into D9.

Possible expressions. Ambivalence toward authority figures without hostility to them; a sense of self that does not announce itself; the possibility that time, money or energy goes abroad, into study, or into things that do not return directly; a father or father-figure connected to belief, distance or education. All of these are contingent; none is required.

Mercury

Technical. 0°16′53″ Taurus — the very first degree of the sign — 9th bhava, Krittika pada 2, nakshatra lord Sun. Friend’s sign. Direct, 23°13′ from the Sun, not combust. Navamsa Capricorn. Dig bala 12.7/60, the second lowest in the chart. Baladi avastha Mrita. Lord of the 1st and the 10th. Conjunct Sun, Mars, Venus; aspected by Saturn.

Classical. Lagna lord and 10th lord in one body, placed in the 9th with the 9th lord: this is the raja yoga engine of the chart, treated in section 9. But two counterweights are on the record. Mercury sits at 0°16′, a sandhi degree at the junction of signs, which classical texts read as a graha not fully established in its rashi. And its baladi avastha is Mrita, the weakest of the five. Neither cancels a yoga; both temper the ease with which it operates.

Possible expressions. Intelligence directed at meaning rather than mechanics; a working life shaped around what one believes about a field; skill in language, analysis or systems used in service of a larger orientation; a slow start in establishing professional footing relative to the underlying capacity. The gap between capacity and establishment is a dasha matter.

Venus

Technical fact

28°01′11″ Taurus, 9th bhava, Mrigashira pada 2, nakshatra lord Mars. OWN SIGN. Retrograde — which Shadbala counts as a source of cheshta bala, i.e. as STRENGTH, not weakness. Against that: combust at 4°32′ from the Sun against a retrograde orb of 8°; dig bala 9.0/60, the lowest of any graha here; DEBILITATED in the navamsa, falling to Virgo; and baladi avastha Bala, a minor modifier. Own sign again in D7 (vargottama) and in D10. Lord of the 9th and the 2nd.

Classical delineation

This is the most important nuance in the chart and it cuts against the obvious reading. Venus holds the single best rashi dignity available — its own sign, as lord of the best trikona, in that trikona. By rashi alone it is the chart’s strongest body. Three further measures cut against it: asta (combust), minimal dig bala, and neecha in D9, with infant avastha a minor fourth. Retrogression is not among them. Shadbala treats a retrograde graha as gaining cheshta bala; reading retrogression as weakening is a modern convention this document does not use. Nor is the picture uniform across the vargas: Venus returns to its own sign in D10 and to own sign plus vargottama in D7. Parashari method does not let rashi dignity overrule the rest, but it equally does not let one varga stand for all of them.

Possible lived expressions
  • Real aesthetic capacity and real taste, which nonetheless may not feel reliably available or self-evident from the inside
  • Value, beauty and relationship mattering enormously as themes while being complicated in operation
  • Fortune arriving through periods rather than as a standing condition — and note that the current antardasha is Venus’s, running to December 2028
  • A gap between how much beauty matters to her and how much she experiences herself as having it
  • Strength that shows up in some contexts and not others, since the vargas genuinely disagree: weak in the ninth-harmonic reading of the chart, strong in the career and progeny vargas

None of this says the capacity is absent. It says the chart makes its availability conditional, and section 13 is where the conditions live.

Drive that attaches to belief or to a field rather than to competition; a capacity for sustained effort that is more available in practice than it appears on paper; friction around inheritance, shared resources, or the fortunes of the father; energy that shows up under pressure rather than on demand.

Jupiter

Technical. 22°05′33″ Sagittarius, 4th bhava, Purva Ashadha pada 3, nakshatra lord Venus. OWN SIGN. Retrograde. Navamsa Libra — own-sign status is not retained. Dig bala 30.1/60. Kendradhipati dosha

Two mechanisms, and they are separate. The general rule (BPHS ch. 34 vv. 2–7) is muting rather than reversal: “Benefics owning Kendras will not give benefic effects, while malefics owning Kendras will not remain inauspicious.” A benefic loses beneficence; it does not thereby become a malefic. Separately, the 7th is a maraka house, which is a distinct liability rather than the same one restated.

But for this lagna the text goes further. Ch. 34 vv. 31–32 names Guru a malefic for Kanya outright. So the label is not an over-reading of kendradhipati dosha, as an earlier audit of this document supposed; it is stated. Both hold at once — the general mechanism mutes, the per-lagna list classes. The chart’s single Mahapurusha yoga is formed by a graha the text calls a malefic for this ascendant. Own-sign placement mitigates; it does not remove.

Possible expressions. A strong attachment to home, land or domestic ground as a source of meaning; wisdom that arrives through the domestic and the private rather than the public; the possibility that expansion in one area of life reliably costs something in another, given the same graha rules both home and partnership. Jupiter’s mahadasha ran 2002 to 2018 — childhood through age twenty-two — so much of its work is already historical rather than pending.

What is not computed here, and why

Full Shadbala requires Sthana, Dig, Kala, Cheshta, Naisargika and Drik bala. Several of those — Cheshta bala in particular — have competing formulations across authorities, and the aggregate thresholds by which a graha is called strong or weak differ between schools. Producing a single number would mean picking one formulation and presenting it as settled. Shadbala is therefore not given.

Ishta and Kashta phala derive from Shadbala components and are likewise omitted rather than approximated.

Strongly supported: the 2nd is ruled by an own-sign Venus in a trikona; the Dharma-Karmadhipati and Lakshmi yogas both bear on fortune, Lakshmi confirmed against Phaladeepika ch. 6 v. 28 — though for a Virgo lagna that verse’s two conditions are both carried by Venus alone, which makes it a cheaper formation than it looks. Mixed: Venus’s condition again, and its maraka lordship of the 2nd. D2 tilts toward received rather than self-generated wealth, which is the weakest evidence in this section and is flagged as such.

Career

Strongly supported: career is one of the chart’s most redundantly signified topics. The 10th lord conjoins the 9th lord — a conjunction by rashi, which no bhava convention can disturb — in the 9th lord’s own sign. Jupiter casts the only graha drishti reaching the 10th. And the D10 is the strongest varga examined: five grahas in own sign or exaltation, Saturn vargottama, Venus in own sign in the house of gains.

Convention-dependent in one respect: the claim that the conjunction sits in the 9th bhava does not hold under Sripati, which places Mercury in the 8th while Venus stays in the 9th. The yoga forms either way, since conjunction is a rashi relationship; its ninth-house seating is the part at stake. The accurate statement is that career is redundantly signified, though the precise ninth-house mechanism is bhava-convention dependent.

Mixed: Mercury as 10th lord is weak in dig bala and Mrita in avastha. The structure is excellent; the vehicle is not effortless.

Friendships and community

Mixed to weak: the 11th is ruled by the Moon, the least connected body in the chart — unaspected, unconjunct, in a dusthana by whole sign. No graha occupies the 11th. This is the thinnest area on the chart’s own evidence, and thin is not the same as bad.

Countervailing: Venus in own sign in the 11th of D10 concerns professional gains rather than friendship, and should not be borrowed to strengthen this reading.

Creativity

Mixed: the 5th is ruled by Saturn — disciplined rather than spontaneous, improving with time, on Saturn’s classical significations. No occupants under either bhava convention, the earlier claim of a Moon in the 5th having been an artefact of the error corrected in section 1. So the 5th is read entirely through its lord, which is thinner evidence than an occupied house would give.

Travel, learning, worldview

Supported, with the one real convention caveat in the document: four grahas in the 9th under whole sign, including the Lagna lord and an own-sign 9th lord. Under Sripati the count drops to two, Sun and Venus, with Mercury and Mars moving to the 8th. The 9th remains the most populated house either way, but “four of nine grahas” is a whole-sign statement and is labelled as one.

Nuance from section 5: the four are not one cluster. Mercury and Mars operate through Krittika under the Sun; the Sun and Venus through Mrigashira under Mars. Two channels, not one.

Spirituality

Mixed: the 9th is strong but is doing dharma-as-orientation work rather than renunciate work. Ketu in the 7th and Saturn’s self-referring nakshatra are the detachment signatures present. The 12th is ruled by the Sun, placed in the 9th — dissolution channelled into belief. No sannyasa yoga forms.

Recurring strengths and recurring tensions

Strengths that appear in more than one layer: the 9th house; Saturn’s non-rashi strength; the career structure; Rahu’s Lagna repetition across three vargas.

Tensions that appear in more than one layer: the gap between rashi dignity and every other measure, most acute in Venus; the separation of the emotional apparatus from the rest of the chart, visible at nakshatra and dispositor level independently; and the fact that both own-sign benefics carry functional complications for this specific Lagna.

Dig bala

Points of maximum strength: Lagna for Jupiter and Mercury, the 4th cusp for Moon and Venus, the 7th for Saturn, the 10th for Sun and Mars. Virupas = (angular distance from the point opposite the point of maximum strength) ÷ 3, giving 0 at the weak point and 60 at the strong one. Computed from the actual ecliptic longitudes of the Ascendant and MC, not from bhava cusps, which is why the dig bala figures are unaffected by the bhava convention.

Baladi avastha

Five states of 6° each within the rashi. In odd rashis the order runs Bala, Kumara, Yuva, Vriddha, Mrita from 0°; in even rashis the order is reversed, so the count runs from 30° downward. State only; the fractional strength multipliers some treatments attach (Bala ¼, Kumara ½, Yuva full, Vriddha ¼, Mrita negligible) were not applied.

Sensitivity of the dasha dates

The year-length convention matters more than it looks. Against 365.25 (Julian year) the difference is under a day across the whole sequence and can be ignored. Against a 360-day savana year, which some traditions use, the first mahadasha boundary moves by roughly 35 days and the error compounds forward.

So the exact dates in section 13 are exact given 365.2425. A practitioner using savana years will get materially different boundaries, and the antardasha and pratyantardasha dates should be read as precise to within days rather than to the day.

What “computed exactly” means and does not mean

Dig bala and baladi avastha are computed exactly in the sense that each has a single unambiguous formula, stated above, applied without approximation. It does not mean the upstream choices are beyond dispute — ayanamsha, node convention, drishti scheme and bhava convention are all choices, and they are listed above precisely so a reader can vary them.
