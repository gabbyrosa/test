# July 2026 Book Releases: Comprehensive Guide (US & UK)

A research-backed roundup of **fiction** publishing in **July 2026** across the US and UK
markets, covering literary/general, commercial/book-club, historical, science fiction &
fantasy, romance & romantasy, thriller/mystery/crime, and horror. Each entry lists author,
publisher (where confirmed), release date and market, and a short synopsis. Built the same
way as the June guide, from full monthly catalogs and UK-specific sources.

**How to read this list**
- Dates are 2026. US street dates are Tuesdays (July 7, 14, 21, 28); UK dates are usually
  Thursdays. (Note: the US July 4 holiday week pushes the first big on-sale day to July 7.)
- *(approx)* means the July month is confirmed but the exact day is not independently verified.
- *(flag)* / *(unconfirmed)* means a publisher, title, or date could not be fully verified,
  or sources disagreed. Cross-market date splits (different US vs UK dates) are noted inline.
- The most anticipated titles are marked with a **star (★)**; the shortlist collects them.
- A note on SFF: the dedicated July SFF roundups (Reactor, Gizmodo) were not yet published
  at research time, so that section leans on Locus and publisher pages and is a little
  thinner than June.
- Sources are grouped at the end.

## 1. Literary & General Fiction

### Big-name / lead titles

- ★ **Cool Machine** by Colson Whitehead (Doubleday). July 21. The final volume of the
  Harlem Trilogy (after *Harlem Shuffle* and *Crook Manifesto*), bringing
  fence-and-furniture-dealer Ray Carney into 1980s New York amid Reagan-era real estate and
  Wall Street excess.

- ★ **Ransom** by Daniel Silva (Harper/HarperCollins). US July 14 / UK July 16. Gabriel
  Allon #26: the retired spy and art restorer hunts for Alice Winter, a British socialite
  who vanishes on holiday, and uncovers dark secrets about her billionaire husband. Reported
  300K first printing.

- ★ **Crash Into Me** by Robinne Lee (St. Martin's Press). July 7. From the author of *The
  Idea of You*: an LA artist literally crashes into Anouk, an enigmatic model she met 20
  years earlier, upending her marriage and identity. Reported 350K first printing.

- ★ **Country People** by Daniel Mason (Random House). July 7 *(LitHub lists July 14)*. From
  the Pulitzer finalist behind *North Woods*: a lyrical year in the life of a family
  striking out into rural Vermont.

- **Beginning Middle End** by Valeria Luiselli (Knopf). July 28. From the author of *Lost
  Children Archive*: a mother and daughter start over, partly in Sicily, exploring lineage.
  Blurbed "maybe perfect" by Tommy Orange.

- **It Will Come Back to You: The Collected Stories** by Sigrid Nunez (Riverhead). July 14.
  The National Book Award winner's first career-spanning short story collection.

- **Yellow Pine** by Claire Vaye Watkins (Riverhead). July 21. Intentional communities,
  corporate resistance, single parenthood, and second-chance romance in the Southwest.

- **Famous Men** by Julie Buntin (Random House). July 21. The second novel from the author
  of *Marlena*: a young woman's obsessive search for a poet from her hometown.

### Other literary / upmarket fiction

- **The Great Wherever** by Shannon Sanders (Henry Holt). July 7. A debut: inheriting a
  Tennessee farm sets off a sweeping family portrait full of secrets and supernatural
  "relentless gossip" dead. NPR/People most-anticipated.

- **Some People** by Parini Shroff (Algonquin). July 7. From the author of *The Bandit
  Queens*: Malti Patel recuperates under the care of her daughter's ex-husband.

- **A Real Animal** by Emeline Atwood (Catapult). July 7. A bildungsroman following a woman
  from a college sexual assault through a decade of relationships.

- **Man Overboard** by Kathleen Rooney. July 7 *(publisher unconfirmed)*. A man lost at sea
  after going overboard on a cruise survives by reliving his memories. Compared to *Where'd
  You Go, Bernadette*.

- **Hustle, Baby** by Priya Guns (Doubleday). July 14. A Tamil refugee family navigates
  precarious work as Y2K approaches and a day trader's promises tempt them.

- **Make Nice** by Ryan Effgen (PRH). July 14. A debut comedy of manners as three
  generations descend on a ritzy Lake Michigan vacation island.

- **Ada** by Mark Haber (Coffee House Press). July 14. A highbrow absurdist novel about a
  petty European tyrant pursuing his married beloved.

- **Seven** by Joanna Kavenna (Faber & Faber). July 14. A philosopher's absurdist quest for
  an ancient game box becomes a journey through human societies to AI.

- **The Half Life** by Rachel Beanland (Simon & Schuster). July 14. A Navy wife's reckoning
  on a remote Italian island during the Atomic Age.

- **Everything to the Sea** by Alicia Upano (William Morrow). July 14. A debut: a young
  couple's budding Hawaii romance deepens after a tsunami strikes.

- ★ **The First House** by Avni Doshi (Hamish Hamilton). UK July 21. From the Booker-shortlisted
  author of *Burnt Sugar*: a woman's seemingly perfect marriage collapses when her husband asks
  for a divorce. A psychodrama of unravelling and rebirth.
- **Ever Land** by Amy Abdelnoor (Hutchinson Heinemann). UK July 9. A literary debut: a grieving
  Jewish teen in 2000 Israel and Safa, a Palestinian girl killed in the Six Day War, stuck in
  an "In-Between."
- **The Mulai** by Munir Hachemi, trans. Julia Sanches (Fitzcarraldo Editions). July. Literary
  SF in translation; post-climate-war colonists on an unnamed planet worship a deity called
  Dog.
- **Exhibition** by Alex Hyde (Granta). July. The second novel from the author of *Violets*.
  (A plot synopsis was not available from the publisher at research time.)
- **Venus, Vanishing** by Rebecca Birrell (Picador). July *(approx)*. In 1928 Berlin, a
  tailor-turned-artist pursues freedom and a reckless affair with her patron; when her
  acclaimed nude "Venus" portraits are exhibited under a man's name, she turns her art into an
  act of revenge.
- **Time to Burn** by Ellery Lloyd (Macmillan). July. A time-travel thriller; commercial time
  travel sells the ultra-rich trips to the 1941 Blitz, and a documentary shoot goes wrong.
- **The Runner** by Scarlett Thomas (Scribner UK). UK July *(US listed November)*. A literary
  thriller; a man with a Bitcoin-priced contract on his life, ultramarathons, and con artists.
- **Murder at the End of the World** by Akane Araki (Pushkin Vertigo). July. Apocalyptic
  thriller, translated from Japanese.
- **Earth 7** by Deb Olin Unferth (Daunt Books Originals). July. Apocalyptic literary fiction.
- **Eden Falls** by Ajay Chowdhury (Harvill). July. A speculative thriller.
- **Henry Tudor Must Die** by Jillian Laine (Michael Joseph). July. Historical romantasy.
- **Lonely Mouth** by Jacqueline Maley (Chatto & Windus). July 2. Tightly controlled Matilda
  works at one of Sydney's buzziest restaurants and rigidly manages her cravings until her
  younger half-sister crashes back into town, dredging up the family's chaotic past. On
  sisterhood, secrets, and food.
- **The Man** by Laura Sims (Verve Books, UK / Putnam, US). July. Domestic suspense.
- **Natural Disaster** by Lisa Owens (Virago). July. Over a single 24-hour period, a darkly
  funny novel charts one woman's increasingly desperate attempts to spend one last perfect day
  with her children before returning to her office job after maternity leave.

## Notes on scope and reliability

- This guide synthesizes a six-agent research sweep across editorial roundups (CrimeReads,
  LitHub, Book Riot, Library Journal Prepub Alert), aggregators (Goodreads monthly lists,
  romance.io, Fresh Fiction, Fearsome Fiction), UK trade sources (The Bookseller, Waterstones,
  Faber, Fitzcarraldo), and individual publisher/author pages.
- Entries marked *(approx)*, *(flag)*, or *(unconfirmed)* should be double-checked on a
  publisher page before being relied on for anything time-sensitive.
- Cross-market date splits are common in July (Silva, Lapena, Howard, Jewell, Ellroy,
  Eggers, Kim); both dates are noted where found.
- SFF coverage is a little thinner than June because the dedicated July SFF roundups had not
  published at research time; it should be re-checked in early July.
- Coverage is fiction (literary/general, commercial/book-club, historical, plus genre). It
  does not attempt nonfiction, YA/children's beyond crossover titles, poetry, or every
  regional small-press release.
