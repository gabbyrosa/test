# June 2026 Book Releases: Comprehensive Guide (US & UK)

A research-backed roundup of **fiction** publishing in **June 2026** across the US and
UK markets, covering literary/general fiction, commercial/book-club fiction, historical
fiction, science fiction & fantasy, romance & romantasy, thriller/mystery/crime, and
horror. Each entry lists author, publisher (where confirmed), release date and market,
and a short synopsis.

This is the expanded edition: it pulls from full monthly catalogs and UK-specific sources
(The Bookseller, Waterstones, Faber, Fitzcarraldo, Gollancz, and others), not just
editorial "best of" lists, and runs to roughly 250 titles.

**How to read this list**
- Dates are 2026. US street dates are Tuesdays (June 2, 9, 16, 23, 30); UK dates are
  usually Thursdays.
- *(approx)* means the June month is confirmed but the exact day is not independently verified.
- *(unconfirmed)* / *(flag)* means a publisher, title, or date could not be fully verified,
  or sources disagreed. Conflicts between sources are noted inline.
- A genuinely complete list of every novel from every imprint would run to many hundreds
  of titles; this aims to be the most comprehensive practical guide, weighted toward titles
  with real sourcing.
- Sources are grouped at the end.

Throughout the genre sections below, the most anticipated titles are marked with a
**star (★)**. The curated shortlist immediately below collects them in one place.

## 1. Literary & General Fiction

### Lead titles

- ★ **Whistler** by Ann Patchett (Harper, US / Bloomsbury, UK). US June 2; UK June 4.
  Daphne Fuller, an English teacher in her fifties, reconnects with a former stepfather
  (Eddie Triplett) after a chance encounter at the Met, across two timelines. A
  meditation on impermanence and connection that defies time. 500K first printing; Katie
  Couric Book Club pick.

- ★ **Land** by Maggie O'Farrell (Knopf, US / Tinder Press, UK). June 2. From the author of
  *Hamnet*: in 1865 Ireland, Tomas surveys the land for the British Ordnance Survey, then
  quits after a mystical encounter to map it "how it really is," sparking a
  multi-generational saga running from the Great Hunger to Canada and India.

- ★ **Villa Coco** by Andrew Sean Greer (Doubleday, US / Hodder & Stoughton/Sceptre, UK).
  June 9. From the Pulitzer winner behind *Less*, a warm, madcap tale of a young American
  who takes a mysterious job helping a charismatic elderly Baronessa at her crumbling
  villa in the Tuscan hills.

- ★ **Contrapposto** by Dave Eggers (Knopf). June 9. Twenty years in the making and drawing
  on Eggers's own art-school years, the 65-year friendship, partnership, and love affair
  of artists Cricket Dib and Olympia Argyros.

- ★ **The Typing Lady: And Other Fictions** by Ruth Ozeki (Viking). June 2. Ozeki's first
  story collection: eleven stories of characters at life's thresholds, grappling with
  shifting identities and the compromises that shape a life.

- **Children of the Wild** by Kevin Powers (Little, Brown / HarperCollins). June 9. From
  the author of *The Yellow Birds*, a haunting love story spanning the Virginia mountains
  and the WWI battlefields of France.

- **Drayton and MacKenzie** by Alexander Starritt (Grove Atlantic, US / Swift Press, UK).
  June 2. Two Oxford classmates, an overachiever who cannot connect and a charming
  self-saboteur, partner during the 2008 crash to chase the enigma of tidal power.

- **Pool House** by Mary H.K. Choi (Henry Holt/Macmillan, US / Phoenix, UK). June 9. The
  bestselling YA author's adult debut: a young woman and her unemployed actress mother
  move into a pool house against a Hollywood backdrop. On mothers, daughters, and family.

- **The Top of the World** by Ethan Joella (Scribner). June 30. A young woman searches for
  answers about her brother's last days.

- **New novel (title unconfirmed)** by Sarah Winman (G.P. Putnam's Sons). June 16. Set in
  1940s Cornwall: the unlikely friendship between an eighty-nine-year-old woman near the
  end of her life and a young soldier recovering from WWII trauma. *(The circulating title
  "A Year of Marvelous Ways" duplicates Winman's 2015 book and appears to be an error.)*

## Notes on scope and reliability

- This guide synthesizes a two-wave, ten-agent research sweep across editorial roundups
  (CrimeReads, Reactor/Tor, Book Riot, LitHub, Gizmodo), aggregators (Goodreads monthly
  lists, romance.io, Library Journal), UK trade sources (The Bookseller, Waterstones,
  Faber), and individual publisher/author pages.
- Verified headline corrections are baked in (see sections 1 and 9). Entries marked
  *(approx)*, *(unconfirmed)*, or *(flag)* should be double-checked on a publisher page
  before being relied on for anything time-sensitive.
- UK exact street dates remain thinner than US, since several UK previews confirm only the
  month. Those are flagged.
- Coverage is fiction (literary/general, commercial/book-club, historical, plus genre). It
  does not attempt nonfiction, YA/children's beyond crossover titles, poetry, or every
  regional small-press release.
