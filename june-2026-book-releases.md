# June 2026 Book Releases: Comprehensive Guide (US & UK)

A research-backed roundup of **fiction** publishing in **June 2026** across both the
US and UK markets, covering literary/general fiction and genre fiction (science
fiction, fantasy, romance, thriller/mystery/crime, and horror). Each entry lists
the author, publisher (where confirmed), release date and market, and a short
synopsis.

**How to read this list**
- Dates are 2026. Where only "June" is given, the source did not specify a day.
- US street dates fall on Tuesdays (June 2, 9, 16, 23, 30); UK dates are typically Thursdays.
- Many titles publish simultaneously (or near-simultaneously) in both markets. UK-specific
  dates are noted where confirmed; otherwise assume the US date carries into the UK in the
  same window unless flagged.
- Items marked *(unconfirmed)* had a detail (usually publisher, exact date, or title) that
  could not be fully verified. They are included for completeness with the caveat noted.
- Sources are listed at the end.

## Literary & General Fiction

### Lead titles

- **Whistler** by Ann Patchett (Harper, US / Bloomsbury, UK). US June 2; UK June 4.
  Patchett's 11th book follows Daphne Fuller, an English teacher in her fifties who
  reconnects with a former stepfather after a chance encounter at the Metropolitan
  Museum of Art. A luminous meditation on the impermanence of life and the connection
  that can defy time.

- **Land** by Maggie O'Farrell (Doubleday, US / Penguin Random House UK). June 2.
  From the author of *Hamnet*, a multi-generational historical epic set on a windswept
  peninsula in the west of Ireland, following its characters through loss, hope, and
  reunion in the years before and after the Great Hunger.

- **Villa Coco** by Andrew Sean Greer (Doubleday, US / Sceptre, UK). June 9.
  From the Pulitzer winner behind *Less*, a warm, madcap tale of a young American who
  takes a job helping a charismatic elderly Baronessa at her crumbling villa in the
  Tuscan hills.

- **Contrapposto** by Dave Eggers (Penguin Random House, US). June 9.
  Tracks the 65-year friendship, partnership, and love affair of artists Cricket Dib
  and Olympia Argyros from childhood to global art careers, told with Eggers' signature
  humor and precision.

- **Children of the Wild** by Kevin Powers (HarperCollins, US). June 9.
  A love story spanning the Virginia mountains and the World War I battlefields of
  France, from the author of *The Yellow Birds*.

- **Drayton and MacKenzie** by Alexander Starritt (Grove Atlantic, US / Swift Press, UK).
  June 2. Two Oxford classmates, an overachiever who cannot connect and a charming
  self-saboteur, form an unlikely partnership during the 2008 crash and chase the
  enigma of tidal power, building an extraordinary friendship and business.

- **New novel (title unconfirmed)** by Sarah Winman (G.P. Putnam's Sons, US). June 16.
  Set in 1940s Cornwall: the unlikely friendship between an eighty-nine-year-old woman
  near the end of her life and a young soldier recovering from World War II trauma, from
  the author of *Still Life*. *(The title circulating in some listings, "A Year of
  Marvelous Ways," duplicates Winman's 2015 book and appears to be an error; treat the
  title as unconfirmed.)*

- **The Children** by Melissa Albert (William Morrow / HarperCollins). June 2. From the
  author of *The Hazel Wood*, the adult children of a legendary author, written into
  their late mother's fantasy series, confront questions of legacy, memory, and magic.
  A literary/gothic blur of fantasy, horror, and mystery.

- **Summerland Cove** by Ellen Baker (HarperCollins). June 2. Family secrets, summer
  celebrations, and reconciliation set in coastal Maine.

- **They All Fall in Love at the End** by Haili Blassingame (Simon & Schuster). June 2.

- **The Jellyfish Problem** by Tessa Yang (Berkley). June 2. A marine biologist

- **Nymph** by Sofia Montrone (Simon & Schuster). June 9. A coming-of-age debut

- **The Lowe Job** by Grace Alexander (HarperCollins). June 16. A stylish debut following

- **A Year of Marvelous Ways** *(see Sarah Winman note above; likely a mislabeled entry).*

- **Arrivals and Departures** by Amanda Eyre Ward *(publisher unconfirmed)*. June 16. A
  witty family saga following the scattered Perkins family across the globe as they
  navigate heartbreak and try to reconnect.

- **Skin Contact** by Elisa Faison *(publisher unconfirmed)*. June 23. A couple navigates
  a newly open marriage as the protagonist, reeling from her mother's death, seeks to
  rediscover purpose and joy.

- **The Shampoo Effect** by Jenny Jackson (Pamela Dorman Books / Little, Brown). June 30.
  An ambitious woman insinuates herself into a tight-knit social group in a small seaside

- **Main Characters** by Bobby Palmer (Little, Brown). June 30. A love story told by
  everyone except the main characters themselves.

- **Short story collection (untitled)** by Lucy Caldwell *(Faber, likely; unconfirmed)*.

## Science Fiction & Fantasy

### Lead / most anticipated

- **The Unicorn Hunters** by Katherine Arden (Del Rey). June 2. Historical fantasy
  following Anne of Brittany, sovereign duchess of an occupied realm. To resist
  annexation she arranges a secret betrothal to France's rival and plans a unicorn hunt
  into an enchanted forest where mythical creatures may still exist.

- **Sublimation** by Isabel J. Kim (Tor Books). June 2. A heavily hyped debut set in a
  world where immigrating means leaving a duplicate of yourself behind. When her
  grandfather dies, Soyoung Rose Kang returns to Korea and finds the copy who stayed is
  planning to take over her life.

- **The Heart of the Nhaga** (The Bird That Drinks Tears #1) by Lee Young-do, trans.
  Anton Hur (Harper Voyager). June 2. First English translation of a classic of Korean
  fantasy, featuring castles built on flying mantas, giant birdmen, and a world-changing
  quest. A major translated SFF event.

- **Songs of the Dead** (The Strata Wars #1) by Brandon Sanderson and Peter Orullian
  (Saga Press / S&S). June 16. A deceased musician discovers that light and music create
  magic in the layered past beneath London, where angry spirits wage revolution against
  the living.

- **Six of Crows: A Darker Shore** by Leigh Bardugo *(date within June unconfirmed)*.
  Set years after a violent upheaval in Ketterdam, two investigators uncover the truth
  behind a massacre, with evidence including correspondence between Kaz Brekker and an
  anonymous sailor. A companion volume, *Letters from Ketterdam*, accompanies it.

### Science fiction

- **Green City Wars** by Adrian Tchaikovsky (Tor Books). June 23. Noir SF where
  genetically engineered animals serve humans; a raccoon private investigator takes a
  missing-mouse case. Pitched as "Philip Marlowe meets Redwall."

- **Exodus: The Helium Sea** by Peter F. Hamilton (Del Rey, US / Tor UK, Pan Macmillan).
  June 16, simultaneous US/UK. Conclusion of the far-future Archimedes Engine duology:
  the Crown Dominion faces an exiled faction returning after seven thousand years to
  claim vengeance. Roughly 752 pages.

- **The Sixth Nik** by Daniel Kraus (Saga Press / S&S). June 23. Epic space SF/horror
  aboard "The Sickness," a ship woven from biomatter. A tech-enhanced nine-year-old and
  her crew investigate a plague-ravaged planet as the ship itself mutates.

- **The Traveler** by Joseph Eckert (Tor Books). June 9. A reluctant time traveler
  experiences spontaneous time slips at accelerating intervals; his son studies quantum
  physics to rescue him.

- **Voyagers** by Meg Charlton (Harper). June 16. Two childhood alien abductees reunite
  when a mysterious "Signal" from the edge of the solar system threatens Earth.

- **Retro** by Jessica M. Goldstein (Ballantine Books). June 23. Agents guide wealthy
  tourists through history until the protagonist's memories and reality unravel.

- **Mr. Yay** by Emily Jane *(publisher unconfirmed)*. June 2. People process
  inexplicable global shifts when personal memories contradict established history.

- **Valet** by J.P. Lacrampe *(publisher unconfirmed)*. June 2. A helper robot and his
  ward go on a madcap adventure to save the family company; a speculative ode to
  Wodehouse's Jeeves and Wooster.

- **Moss'd in Space** by Rebecca Thorne *(publisher unconfirmed)*. June 30. A new
  spaceship owner discovers the moss covering the vessel is a snarky organic computer
  with abandonment issues. Cozy SF.

- **Lucky Starr and the Pirates of the Asteroids** by Isaac Asimov (Blackstone). June 9.
  Reissue of the classic juvenile SF.

## Notes on scope and reliability

- **Verified headline corrections:** *You First* (Kepnes) confirmed June 9 with corrected
  synopsis; *Hunger and Thirst* (Fuller) confirmed Tin House, June 2, and is a single
  literary/gothic-horror title; the Sarah Winman June 16 title is unconfirmed (the
  circulating title duplicates her 2015 book); *The Hallmarked Man* is a June 2026
  paperback, not a new novel.
- **UK coverage** is thinner than US in source roundups. Several US titles publish in the
  UK in the same window but without separately confirmed UK street dates.
- Entries marked *(unconfirmed)* or *(uncertain)* should be double-checked against a
  publisher page before being relied on for anything time-sensitive.
- This list emphasizes fiction (literary/general plus genre). It does not attempt to cover
  nonfiction, YA/children's, or every regional small-press title.
