**Key per-album trivia:**
- **Taylor Swift (2006):** Debut. Recorded at age 16. Lead single "Tim McGraw."
- **Fearless (2008):** Won Album of the Year at the 2010 Grammys (she was the youngest solo AOTY winner at the time, age 20). Platinum Edition added 6 songs.
- **Speak Now (2010):** Every track written entirely solo by Swift.
- **Red (2012):** Her first major pop crossover; mixes country and pop. Features the original 5-minute "All Too Well."
- **1989 (2014):** Her full pop pivot; named after her birth year. Won Album of the Year at the 2016 Grammys.
- **reputation (2017):** Her last album under Big Machine.
- **Lover (2019):** First album she fully owns the masters to; first on Republic Records.
- **folklore (2020):** Surprise pandemic album, indie-folk sound; won Album of the Year at the 2021 Grammys. Deluxe bonus track: "the lakes."
- **evermore (2020):** "Sister album" to folklore, released less than five months later. Bonus tracks: "right where you left me," "it's time to go."
- **Midnights (2022):** Concept of "13 sleepless nights." The *3am Edition* added 7 tracks; the *Til Dawn Edition* added "Hits Different." Won Album of the Year at the 2024 Grammys.
- **The Tortured Poets Department (2024):** Released as a surprise double album. *The Anthology* expansion (31 tracks total) dropped about two hours after the standard edition.
- **The Life of a Showgirl (2025):** 12th studio album; reunited with Max Martin and Shellback. Title/closing track features Sabrina Carpenter. Became the fastest-selling album in history (4+ million first-week units).

## 3. Taylor's Versions (Re-Recordings)

After the masters dispute (Section 7), Swift began re-recording her first six albums to own new masters. Four have been released:

| Re-recording | Release date | "From the Vault" tracks |
|---|---|---|
| **Fearless (Taylor's Version)** | April 9, 2021 | 6 |
| **Red (Taylor's Version)** | November 12, 2021 | **9** |
| **Speak Now (Taylor's Version)** | July 7, 2023 | 6 |
| **1989 (Taylor's Version)** | October 27, 2023 | 5 |

> **Trivia Trap — Red (TV) vault count:** The correct From the Vault count is **9**, not 6. The nine consist of six brand-new unreleased songs, the 10-minute version of "All Too Well," plus Swift's own versions of "Better Man" and "Babe" (songs she originally wrote for other artists). Many summaries undercount this.

**1989 (TV) vault tracks (5):** "Slut!", "Say Don't Go," "Now That We Don't Talk," "Suburban Legends," "Is It Over Now?"

> **Trivia Trap — the two not re-recorded:** As of early 2026, the two original albums NOT released as Taylor's Versions are her **self-titled debut *Taylor Swift* (2006)** and ***reputation* (2017)**. In **June 2025 she bought back the masters to all six original Big Machine albums**, which removed the financial pressure to finish those last two re-recordings.

**Grammys:**
- 4 Album of the Year wins is the **all-time record** for a lead artist: **Fearless** (2010), **1989** (2016), **folklore** (2021), **Midnights** (2024).
- She won her first AOTY at age 20, the youngest solo winner at the time (record later broken by Billie Eilish at 18 in 2020).
- With *The Tortured Poets Department*, she became the first woman with 7 AOTY nominations (she lost the 2025 award to Beyoncé).

**Billboard:**
- Midnights made her the **first artist to occupy the entire top 10 of the Hot 100** simultaneously (chart dated Nov 5, 2022), with "Anti-Hero" at No.1.
- She later took the **top 14** with TTPD (2024) and the **top 12** with *The Life of a Showgirl* (2025).
- 15 No.1 albums on the Billboard 200, the most of any soloist (she passed Jay-Z).
- "Anti-Hero" spent **8 weeks at No.1**, her longest-running chart-topper.

**Streaming:**
- *The Tortured Poets Department* set Spotify single-day records on April 19, 2024 (most-streamed artist, album, and song in a day) and was the first album to top 300 million Spotify streams in a single day.

**Other awards & milestones:**
- **40 AMA wins** make her the most-awarded artist in American Music Awards history (she passed Michael Jackson in 2019).
- **5 VMA Video of the Year** wins (the record): "Bad Blood" (2015), "You Need to Calm Down" (2019), *All Too Well: The Short Film* (2022), "Anti-Hero" (2023), "Fortnight" (2024).
- **TIME Person of the Year 2023** — the first entertainer named solo.
- Reached **billionaire status in October 2023** (per Bloomberg/Forbes), largely on music and the Eras Tour.

## 5. Songwriting, Collaborators & "Who Songs Are About"

### Key collaborators
- **Liz Rose** — Early Nashville co-writer. Co-wrote "Tim McGraw," "Teardrops on My Guitar" (debut), "White Horse" and "You Belong With Me" (Fearless). "White Horse" won the 2010 Grammy for Best Country Song.
- **Max Martin & Shellback** — Swedish hitmakers behind the big pop singles: "We Are Never Ever Getting Back Together," "I Knew You Were Trouble," "22" (Red), and "Shake It Off," "Blank Space," "Bad Blood" (1989). Reunited with her for *The Life of a Showgirl* (2025).
- **Jack Antonoff** — Her most frequent modern collaborator, from 1989 ("Out of the Woods") onward through Lover, folklore, evermore, Midnights ("Anti-Hero"), and TTPD.
- **Aaron Dessner** (of The National) — Produced and co-wrote the bulk of the "sister albums" folklore and evermore, and continued on Midnights and TTPD.
- **"William Bowery"** — Pen name for then-boyfriend **Joe Alwyn**; co-wrote "exile" and "betty" (folklore), "champagne problems," "coney island," "evermore" (evermore), and "Sweet Nothing" (Midnights). Revealed in the *Long Pond Studio Sessions* film.

### Pen name "Nils Sjöberg"
Swift used the pseudonym **Nils Sjöberg** on **"This Is What You Came For"** (Calvin Harris ft. Rihanna, 2016), which she co-wrote while dating Harris. The fake name kept dual star billing from distracting from the song; it was revealed after the Swift-Harris breakup.

### Solo-written songs
- **Speak Now (2010) is entirely solo-written** — every track. This is the headline fact.
- Other notable fully solo compositions include "Love Story," "Fifteen," "The Best Day" (Fearless), "This Love" (1989), "Lover," "Cornelia Street," "Daylight" (Lover), "my tears ricochet" (folklore), and "Vigilante Shit" (Midnights).

### "Who is it about" (handle with care)
> Swift rarely names song subjects. Outside of a couple of cases, these are media/fan interpretations, not her confirmations.

| Song(s) | Associated subject | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "Forever & Always," "Last Kiss" | Joe Jonas | Speculated (strongly) |
| "Dear John" | John Mayer | Speculated; Mayer himself addressed it in a 2012 Rolling Stone interview, saying it "humiliated" him |
| "All Too Well" (and the Red era) | Jake Gyllenhaal | Speculated (strongly); never confirmed by Swift |
| "Style," "Out of the Woods" | Harry Styles | Speculated |
| Much of Lover; parts of folklore/evermore/TTPD | Joe Alwyn | Relationship confirmed; Alwyn confirmed as co-writer "William Bowery"; specific song subjects interpretive |
| Much of TTPD | Matty Healy | Speculated |
| "The Alchemy," "So High School" (TTPD) | Travis Kelce | Relationship confirmed; songs are fan theory |

### Feuds reflected in songs
- **"Bad Blood"** — In a 2014 Rolling Stone cover, Swift said it was about a fellow female artist who tried to sabotage her tour. Widely reported as **Katy Perry** (a dispute over poached backup dancers). They reconciled: Perry appeared in the "You Need to Calm Down" video (2019).
- **Kanye West arc** — The 2009 VMA stage interruption is confirmed. West's "Famous" (2016) and its leaked phone call were the flashpoint; the full 2020 leak showed Swift approved being name-dropped but was not told about the derogatory line. On TTPD, **"thanK you aIMee"** has capital letters spelling **KIM** (Kim Kardashian), widely read as a diss.

## 6. Concert Tours

| Tour | Years | Supporting Album | Total Gross | Shows |
|------|-------|-----------------|-------------|-------|
| Fearless Tour | 2009-2010 | *Fearless* | ~$66.5M | 89 reported |
| Speak Now World Tour | 2011-2012 | *Speak Now* | ~$123.7M | 110 |
| The Red Tour | 2013-2014 | *Red* | $150.2M | 86 |

| The Eras Tour | 2023-2024 | career retrospective | **$2,077,618,725** | 149 |

**Tour highlights:**
- **Red Tour:** Became the highest-grossing country tour of all time at the time.
- **1989 World Tour:** World's highest-grossing tour of 2015.
- **reputation Stadium Tour:** Set the record for highest-grossing US tour at the time ($266.1M domestic), beating the Rolling Stones' previous record.
- **The Eras Tour:**
  - Opened **March 17, 2023** at State Farm Stadium in Glendale, Arizona (the city was ceremonially renamed "Swift City" for the opening). Closed **December 8, 2024** at BC Place in Vancouver.
  - **149 shows in 51 cities across five continents.**
  - Final gross **$2.077 billion** on 10,168,008 sold-out attendance. The **first tour in history to pass $1 billion** (Dec 2023) and then **$2 billion**, roughly double the previous record.
  - Set list is a ~3.5-hour, ~44-46 song retrospective organized into **10 "eras."**
  - **Surprise songs concept:** Two surprise acoustic songs each night, one on guitar and one on piano, generally not repeated. These often became fan-tracked mashups.

**The Eras Tour Concert Film (2023):**
- Released October 13, 2023, distributed by **AMC Theatres** (bypassing traditional studios).
- Became the **highest-grossing concert film of all time** (final worldwide run ~$267M), passing *Michael Jackson's This Is It*.
- An extended **Taylor's Version** with extra songs was released on **Disney+ on March 14, 2024**.

## 7. Easter Eggs, Lore, Masters, Videos & Acting

### Lucky number 13
She was **born December 13, 1989**, and **turned 13 on Friday the 13th**, which cemented 13 as her lucky number. During the Fearless Tour she painted "13" on the back of her hand before each show.

### Hidden messages
In her printed CD lyric booklets, Swift hid secret messages by **capitalizing select letters** to spell words, on every studio album from her **debut (2006) through Red (2012)**. Examples: "Tim McGraw" spelled "Can't tell me nothin'"; "Fifteen" spelled "I cried while recording this." For **1989 (2014)** she inverted the system, using lowercase letters within all-caps text. This evolved into her broader modern "Easter egg" culture of clues in videos, captions, and dates.

### Cats
Three cats, all named after TV/film characters:
- **Meredith Grey** (Scottish Fold) — after Dr. Meredith Grey on *Grey's Anatomy*.
- **Olivia Benson** (Scottish Fold) — after Det. Olivia Benson on *Law & Order: SVU*.
- **Benjamin Button** (Ragdoll) — after the title character in *The Curious Case of Benjamin Button*; she met him on the set of the "ME!" video.

### The masters dispute
- **2005:** A 15-year-old Swift signed with **Big Machine Records**, which retained the masters to her first six albums.
- **June 2019:** **Scooter Braun's Ithaca Holdings acquired Big Machine** (~$330M), gaining those masters. Swift publicly objected.
- **October 2020:** Braun sold the masters to **Shamrock Capital** (reported ~$405M).
- **November 2020:** Swift began re-recording as "(Taylor's Version)."
- **May 30, 2025:** Swift announced she **bought back all her original masters from Shamrock**, regaining her catalog, videos, and unreleased material.

> **Trivia Trap — buyback date:** The masters buyback was announced **May 30, 2025**, not late 2025. The purchase price was not officially disclosed.

### Music videos & directing
- Self-directed videos include "The Man," "cardigan," "willow," "Anti-Hero," "Lavender Haze," "Karma," "Fortnight," and "All Too Well: The Short Film."
- For **"The Man" (2020)** she became the first solo woman to win Best Direction at the VMAs.
- **"All Too Well: The Short Film" (2021)** is about 15 minutes long, stars **Sadie Sink and Dylan O'Brien**, and won the 2023 Grammy for Best Music Video.

### Acting & screen roles
- **Valentine's Day** (2010) — acting debut.
- **The Giver** (2014) — plays Rosemary.
- **Cats** (2019) — plays Bombalurina.
- **Amsterdam** (2022) — David O. Russell ensemble film.
- **Miss Americana** (2020) — Netflix documentary.
- **folklore: the long pond studio sessions** (2020) — concert documentary she co-directed.

### Recent events (through early 2026)
- **Engagement:** Swift and **Travis Kelce announced their engagement on August 26, 2025** ("Your English teacher and your gym teacher are getting married"). They began dating in 2023, and her NFL/Chiefs appearances were a major storyline.
- **The Life of a Showgirl** (Oct 3, 2025) became the fastest-selling album in history.
